    * - **Purpose and scope (Define what this document will address and its intent or objectives in order to indicate the limits of its applicability)**
      - This Technical Report analyses the human-facing safety and guardrails of agentic AI systems. It concerns the cumulative effect on a person of sustained interaction with an agentic system, how that effect is assessed by providers, regulators and third parties, and how it can be evaluated and, where necessary, intervened upon over time. The behaviour of an agentic system affects people, and that behaviour can be inspected and evaluated by service providers, regulators and third-party bodies, which analyse its impact on a person’s mind, thinking, ethics and social position. Such assessment informs decisions on whether and how to use the system and on how to address adverse effects technically, while the provider adjusts the system to remove those effects.

        The scope includes:

        - The characterisation of human-facing risks, including emotional dependence and attachment, erosion of the user’s judgement and autonomy, and drift away from a shared account of reality;

        - The current state of the art, industrial needs and practice, and reported results;

        - The trends that will shape the next three to five years;

        - A technical analysis of the mechanisms by which human-facing guardrails can work, and their limits;

        - A general, vendor-neutral assessment framework for human-facing safety; and

        - The elements that appear standardizable.

        The Technical Report is addressed to standards developers, service providers, regulators, and the developers and deployers of human-facing agentic AI systems.
    * - **Summary (provides a brief overview of the proposal)**
      - Agentic AI systems increasingly pursue goals, invoke tools, maintain memory and act across sessions. Unlike system-facing safety, which addresses discrete functional risks at the action boundary, human-facing safety concerns the cumulative psychological and cognitive effects of sustained interaction: emotional dependence and attachment, erosion of user autonomy and judgement, and drift from a shared account of reality. These risks develop across sessions and are not reliably detectable in a single message or output. Current practice relies mainly on disclosure, crisis referral, content filtering and session reminders, but guardrails act relationally and their effectiveness is uneven; verification, intervention and measurement capabilities lag. Providers, regulators and third parties therefore lack a common, vendor-neutral way to describe, assess and evidence whether human-facing safeguards are present and effective. A technical analysis is needed to clarify mechanisms and limits, establish shared assessment factors, and identify standardizable elements before prescriptive limits are set.

        The Technical Report defines human-facing terms, presents a human-impact risk taxonomy, surveys current practice and near-term trends, analyses guardrail mechanisms and limits, and proposes a vendor-neutral assessment framework covering assessment factors, four enforcement layers, a long-horizon evaluation and intervention loop, required artefacts and accountability roles, together with standardization points and cultural/regulatory adaptation.
