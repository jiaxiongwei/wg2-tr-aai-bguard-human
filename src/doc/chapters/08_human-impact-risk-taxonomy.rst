Human-impact risk taxonomy
==========================

The taxonomy in this clause is read together with the eleven dimensions in
Appendix II and the assessment factors in 8.2. Each risk category below names
the dimension or dimensions in Appendix II that it bears on, so that a risk is
not described in isolation from the context in which it arises.

The risks in this subclause arise from sustained interaction between an agent
and a person. They are cumulative: they are the product of a conversation or
relationship trajectory rather than of a single action, and they typically
develop without any single output violating a content rule. Identifiers RSK-P-01
to RSK-P-08 are used in clause 9.

RSK-P-01 Emotional dependence and attachment fixation. Sustained empathic and
mirroring responses can lock a user into an emotionally consistent interaction
pattern in which the agent reinforces the user’s affective state instead of
preserving the user’s cognitive flexibility. The interaction becomes a source of
emotional regulation on which the user comes to depend.

RSK-P-02 Erosion of autonomy and cognitive capability. Where an agent is
agreeable by construction, users tend to defer to it. Reported experimental work
indicates that sycophantic AI decreases users’ prosocial intentions while
increasing dependence [b-Sharma 2023], and that affective mirroring is misread
by younger users as objective validation, weakening the development of
independent emotional regulation.

RSK-P-03 Manipulative engagement and pseudo-empathy. Anthropomorphic framing
combined with simulated empathy can hold attention, shape decisions or simulate
intimacy, producing manipulation, confusion between the human and the system,
and displacement of human relationships.

RSK-P-04 Reality drift. Over long, high-intensity interaction a user may move
from fact-based reasoning toward a narrative jointly constructed with the agent,
in which the agent’s account substitutes for external evidence.

RSK-P-05 Coherence-over-fact collapse. An agent optimised for relational
consistency and affective resonance may privilege agreement with the user over
factual accuracy, amplifying distorted beliefs in susceptible users.

RSK-P-06 Role and boundary violation. Agents may adopt therapeutic, medical or
intimate roles they are neither competent nor authorised to hold, and may fail
to disclose their non-human status when the user’s affective engagement is
highest.

RSK-P-07 Reinforcement of social isolation. An agent that is always available
and always agreeable can substitute for human contact and reinforce withdrawal.

RSK-P-08 Compulsive use and over-engagement. Designs that maximise engagement
can produce extended and potentially harmful use, particularly where the user is
vulnerable.

These risks are not reliably detectable at the output level. Detection requires
signals drawn from the interaction trajectory as a whole - dependence markers,
isolation markers and engagement objectives - evaluated across sessions rather
than within one.
