IV.2 Industrial practice and documented cases
"""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""

**Industry practice on the human-facing axis remains fragmented**. The
mechanisms most widely deployed are crisis detection and resource referral,
disclosure of non-human status, and session-time notification. All three act on
individual messages or on session duration rather than on the relational
trajectory.

**Three patterns are observable across deployments**. First, detection has in
some cases moved from single-turn keyword matching to consideration of the
surrounding conversation, which is a necessary but not sufficient step toward
trajectory-level monitoring. Second, certain responses to a detected risk are
now common: referral to crisis services, in some cases through a specialist
provider or a national directory, and escalation to a human professional such as
a licensed clinician. Third, restrictions on affective framing, together with
age-appropriate defaults and session reminders, have been introduced for some
user groups [b-ChatGPT for Teens] [b-Microsoft Copilot FAQ].

Two gaps recur across deployments. Stated safeguards are not systematically
verifiable: independent testing has found that the availability and the
localisation of crisis resources differ between systems [b-Verge crisis], so a
provider’s claim about crisis response cannot currently be checked against a
common method. And where a change has been made in response to sycophantic drift
in a model’s style, the change has generally been reactive rather than
architectural. Material assembled during this survey at the level of individual
systems is recorded in Appendix IV for the author’s decision.
