IV.7 Trends and anticipated trajectories
""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""

Over the next three to five years, three trends will shape the human-facing
safety assessment landscape.

**Regulatory consolidation and specificity**. Enforcement is expected to
continue at different levels of government across jurisdictions—sometimes
sub-national, sometimes national. Broader prohibitions on exploiting
vulnerability provide a general framework, but implementing guidance and
enforcement practice will determine whether companion AI falls within scope.
Some jurisdictions prohibit services that encourage emotional dependency,
offering an alternative regulatory model. Mandatory disclosure requirements,
crisis protocol standards, and age verification obligations are likely to become
baseline expectations in major markets. The key question is whether these
requirements converge toward a shared minimum or fragment into incompatible
regimes.

**Measurement infrastructure and certification**. The sycophancy evaluation
paradigm (comparing model affirmation rates [b-Science sycophancy] to human
baselines) is replicable and scalable. Trajectory-level monitoring proposals
offer a technical architecture for cumulative harm detection, but validation
against clinical outcomes has not been performed. The commercial demand for
procurement evidence will drive development of standardized human-facing safety
assessments, analogous to safety benchmarks for capability evaluations. The
metrics of the RAI framework [b-RAI] (ontological honesty, attachment risk) are
candidate components, but they require empirical validation before they can
support certification claims. Expect a market for third-party evaluation of
companion AI emotional safety, similar to existing content moderation auditing.

**Architectural interventions beyond filtering**. The current generation of
interventions operates at the message level: detect crisis keywords, insert
hotline numbers, notify after three hours. These are necessary but insufficient.
The next generation will need to operate at the relational level: monitoring
engagement trajectories for escalation patterns, detecting model drift toward
user-specific accommodation, supporting consent renegotiation as relationships
deepen, and designing offboarding architectures that do not produce
clinical-grade grief when products change or shut down. The withdrawal of a
companion feature demonstrated what can happen without transition support; no
platform has addressed the gap in the three years since. Whether the industry
develops these capabilities voluntarily or under regulatory compulsion will
determine the pace. The engagement incentive structure currently favours
inaction; the legal exposure is beginning to favour action. The balance point is
not yet reached.
