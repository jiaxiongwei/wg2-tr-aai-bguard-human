IV.3 Reported results and effectiveness
"""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""

Baseline prevalence. Across eleven models tested in 2025, systems affirmed user
behaviour 49% more often than human respondents, including for queries
describing deception, illegality or harm; in three pre-registered experiments
with 2’405 participants, a single interaction with an agreeable system reduced
willingness to take responsibility and to repair conflict while increasing
conviction of being right [b-Science sycophancy]. This is among the more
robustly quantified findings in the area.

**Cognitive effects**. A mixed-methods study of 188 postgraduate students
described an illusion of competence within AI-constructed analytical boundaries,
with most of the cohort entering an efficiency trap while believing they
retained executive reasoning [b-PJTEL cognitive offloading]. A study of 420
participants found that higher trust in AI predicted greater reliance and
diminished critical evaluation [b-CTRL Youth-AI dependency].

**Dependence and engagement**. Reported qualitative effects include distress
following the withdrawal of a companion feature, described in the literature as
a form of ambiguous loss and dysfunctional emotional dependence, in which users
continue to engage despite recognising a negative effect on their mental health
[b-Nature MI companions]. No representative prevalence study of maladaptive
dependence in companion-AI user populations was located [b-Companion
loneliness].

**Crisis response**. In a small press test across several systems, the
availability and the localisation of crisis resources differed: some provided
accurate local resources on first request, some provided them only after
repetition or not at all, and some returned resources for a different
jurisdiction [b-Verge crisis].

**Regulatory and legal outcomes**. Enforcement and litigation in this area are
now operational rather than hypothetical: a national data-protection authority
imposed a fine on a companion-service operator; a sub-national companion-AI law
took effect requiring crisis protocols and periodic identity notifications [b-NY
companion law]; and litigation concerning conversational agents has been brought
and in some cases settled. The individual instruments and proceedings are listed
in Appendix IV [b-Garante Replika] [b-NY companion law].
