IV.5 Documented management strategies of mainstream providers
"""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""""

The systems that reach the largest numbers of users document the management of
model behaviour in two main forms: a public usage or content policy, and a model
or system card. This subclause states the strategies that are described in
public documents; it does not assess their quality.

In the United States, OpenAI publishes usage policies that state which uses are
prohibited and reserve the right to restrict or terminate access for violations
[b-OpenAI usage policies]. Anthropic publishes a Responsible Scaling Policy that
describes rapid-response procedures for jailbreak attempts, including retraining
and classifier validation [b-Anthropic RSP]. Google DeepMind publishes a
Frontier Safety Framework that describes protocols for detecting when a model
attains a defined capability level and for preparing mitigations [b-Google
Frontier Safety].

In China, Alibaba’s AI governance page for Qwen states that content moderation
detects and blocks unsafe prompts and outputs, including material that involves
minors [b-Alibaba AI governance]. ByteDance’s community guidelines for Doubao
prohibit content that harms minors, violates privacy, or promotes violence
[b-ByteDance Doubao guidelines]. DeepSeek’s terms of use state that output is
not professional advice and that expert consultation is expected for medical,
legal or financial decisions [b-DeepSeek terms of use].

On effectiveness, the measured evidence is uneven. The results reported in
Appendix IV.3 concern mainly the systems for which independent evaluation
exists: agreement with the user measured against a human baseline, and the
availability of crisis resources. For the human-facing axis, published measured
effectiveness is concentrated in a small number of systems, and the same
measures are not yet reported across the provider set.
