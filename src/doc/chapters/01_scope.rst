
.. _chapter-scope:

Scope
=====

This Technical Report analyses the human-facing safety and guardrails of agentic
AI systems. It concerns the cumulative effect on a person of sustained
interaction with an agentic system, how that effect is assessed by providers,
regulators and third parties, and how it can be evaluated and, where necessary,
intervened upon over time. The behaviour of an agentic system affects people,
and that behaviour can be inspected and evaluated by service providers,
regulators and third-party bodies, which analyse its impact on a person’s mind,
thinking, ethics and social position. Such assessment informs decisions on
whether and how to use the system and on how to address adverse effects
technically, while the provider adjusts the system to remove those effects.

The scope includes:

-  The characterisation of human-facing risks, including emotional dependence
   and attachment, erosion of the user’s judgement and autonomy, and drift away
   from a shared account of reality;

-  The current state of the art, industrial needs and practice, and reported
   results;

-  The trends that will shape the next three to five years;

-  A technical analysis of the mechanisms by which human-facing guardrails can
   work, and their limits;

-  A general, vendor-neutral assessment framework for human-facing safety; and

-  The elements that appear standardizable.

The Technical Report is addressed to standards developers, service providers,
regulators, and the developers and deployers of human-facing agentic AI systems.
