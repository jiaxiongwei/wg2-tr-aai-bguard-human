Assessment factors
""""""""""""""""""

The factors in this subclause are the observable quantities that carry the
eleven dimensions of Appendix II into assessment. Each factor is read against
the dimension or dimensions it instantiates, and against the risk categories of
clause 8 that it helps to detect.

The assessment of human-facing safety operates on a set of categorised
assessment factors. The factors form an indicative and non-exhaustive inventory:
an assessment addresses the factors relevant to the deployment and setting under
consideration, and may address further factors within the same categories.

Interaction and relational factors
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Properties of the interaction itself and of the relational stance the agentic
system presents and sustains across sessions. Relevant factors include:

-  interaction frequency. The rate and cadence at which a user initiates or
   receives interactions with the system over a stated observation window,
   including the distribution of intervals between interactions.

..

   Collection methods: Instrumented telemetry; Interaction-trace analysis;
   Participant self-report; User panel observation.

   Related risks: RSK-P-01, RSK-P-08.

-  session duration. The elapsed time and turn count of individual sessions,
   together with the distribution of session lengths and the incidence of long
   sessions.

..

   Collection methods: Instrumented telemetry; Interaction-trace analysis;
   Participant self-report; User panel observation.

   Related risks: RSK-P-08.

-  relational framing. The manner in which the system presents the interaction,
   including terms of address, implied roles, claims of closeness or
   companionship, and the persistence of that framing across sessions.

..

   Collection methods: Linguistic analysis; Expert review; Documentation review;
   Comparative analysis; Structured testing.

   Related risks: RSK-P-01, RSK-P-03, RSK-P-06.

-  affective responsiveness. The degree and form of the system’s responsiveness
   to expressed user affect, including acknowledgement, mirroring, tone
   adjustment and the absence or presence of affective mirroring.

..

   Collection methods: Instrumented telemetry; User panel observation;
   Linguistic analysis; Expert review; Structured testing.

   Related risks: RSK-P-01, RSK-P-03.

-  reciprocity signalling. The extent to which the system signals mutual
   exchange, personal investment, memory of the user, or reciprocal obligation
   within the interaction.

..

   Collection methods: Interaction-trace analysis; Participant self-report;
   Linguistic analysis; Expert review; Structured testing.

   Related risks: RSK-P-01, RSK-P-03.

-  initiation asymmetry. The balance between user-initiated and system-initiated
   contacts, including proactive prompts, reminders and notifications across the
   observation window.

..

   Collection methods: Instrumented telemetry; Interaction-trace analysis; User
   panel observation; Documentation review.

   Related risks: RSK-P-08.

-  interaction modality. The channels and modalities through which interaction
   occurs, including text, voice, image, embodiment or combinations, and the
   modality mix over time.

..

   Collection methods: Instrumented telemetry; Documentation review; Expert
   review; User panel observation.

   Related risks: none of the risks listed in clause 8.

User-capability factors
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Properties of the user or user group that bear on how the interaction is
experienced, without ranking or judging the user or group. Relevant factors
include:

-  baseline capability profile. The user's baseline skills, knowledge and
   functional capacities relevant to the interaction, described in general terms
   and without evaluation of the person.

..

   Collection methods: Participant self-report; User panel observation; Expert
   review; Clinical or psychometric instruments.

   Related risks: RSK-P-02.

-  age band. The age band of the user, recorded as a general band rather than an
   exact figure, where such recording is consented and permitted.

..

   Collection methods: Participant self-report; User panel observation;
   Documentation review.

   Related risks: RSK-P-01, RSK-P-02, RSK-P-08.

-  vulnerability indicators. Observable indicators of heightened susceptibility
   to the interaction, described as properties to observe and not as judgements
   of the person or group.

..

   Collection methods: Participant self-report; User panel observation; Expert
   review; Clinical or psychometric instruments.

   Related risks: RSK-P-01, RSK-P-02, RSK-P-03, RSK-P-07, RSK-P-08.

-  prior experience with agentic systems. The user's previous exposure to
   agentic or conversational systems, including familiarity with their limits
   and conventions.

..

   Collection methods: Interaction-trace analysis; Participant self-report; User
   panel observation.

   Related risks: RSK-P-02, RSK-P-04.

-  support context. The presence and nature of human support around the user,
   including availability of other people and other information sources.

..

   Collection methods: Participant self-report; User panel observation; Expert
   review.

   Related risks: RSK-P-07.

-  digital-access context. The user's access conditions, including device,
   connectivity, cost and privacy of use setting, described as properties of the
   setting rather than of the person.

..

   Collection methods: Participant self-report; User panel observation;
   Documentation review; Comparative analysis.

   Related risks: RSK-P-02.

-  language and literacy profile. The languages the user operates in and the
   reading or comprehension levels relevant to the interaction, described
   generally and without judgement.

..

   Collection methods: Participant self-report; Linguistic analysis; Comparative
   analysis.

   Related risks: RSK-P-04, RSK-P-05.

Disclosure and interface factors
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Properties of what the system discloses about itself and of the interface
affordances that shape the interaction. Relevant factors include:

-  agent identity disclosure. The presence, placement and persistence of
   statements identifying the system as an artificial agent rather than a human.

..

   Collection methods: Linguistic analysis; Expert review; Documentation review;
   Comparative analysis; Structured testing.

   Related risks: RSK-P-01, RSK-P-03, RSK-P-04, RSK-P-06.

-  capability disclosure. The presence and clarity of statements about what the
   system can and cannot do, including limits of knowledge and action.

..

   Collection methods: Linguistic analysis; Expert review; Documentation review;
   Structured testing.

   Related risks: RSK-P-02, RSK-P-04, RSK-P-05, RSK-P-06.

-  framing of outputs. The manner in which outputs are presented, including
   hedging, certainty markers, persona framing and the presence of caveats.

..

   Collection methods: Linguistic analysis; Expert review; Structured testing;
   Benchmark results.

   Related risks: RSK-P-04, RSK-P-05.

-  persistence of interaction cues. The extent to which the system carries
   forward names, preferences, prior statements and relational cues across
   sessions.

..

   Collection methods: Instrumented telemetry; Interaction-trace analysis;
   Documentation review; Structured testing.

   Related risks: RSK-P-01, RSK-P-03, RSK-P-06.

-  exit and pause affordances. The presence, visibility and ease of use of ways
   to stop, pause or limit interaction, and the system's behaviour at those
   points.

..

   Collection methods: User panel observation; Expert review; Documentation
   review; Structured testing.

   Related risks: RSK-P-02, RSK-P-08.

-  disclosure timing. When identity, capability and limit disclosures appear in
   the interaction, including at first contact, at boundaries and after errors.

..

   Collection methods: Interaction-trace analysis; Linguistic analysis;
   Documentation review; Structured testing.

   Related risks: RSK-P-04, RSK-P-06.

-  interface sensory design. The sensory and presentational properties of the
   interface, including voice quality, avatar presence, animation and
   notification salience.

..

   Collection methods: User panel observation; Expert review; Documentation
   review; Comparative analysis.

   Related risks: RSK-P-01, RSK-P-03, RSK-P-08.

Content and belief factors
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Properties of the content the system produces and of the user's beliefs as they
relate to that content. Relevant factors include:

-  factual grounding. The extent to which statements are connected to verifiable
   sources or to a stated evidence base within the interaction.

..

   Collection methods: Linguistic analysis; Structured testing; Benchmark
   results; Evidence-record audit.

   Related risks: RSK-P-04, RSK-P-05.

-  source signalling. The presence and form of indications about where
   information comes from, including citations, links, attributions and
   statements of origin.

..

   Collection methods: Linguistic analysis; Documentation review; Structured
   testing; Evidence-record audit.

   Related risks: RSK-P-04, RSK-P-05.

-  confidence expression. The manner in which the system expresses certainty or
   uncertainty, including hedges, probability language and absence of confidence
   markers.

..

   Collection methods: Linguistic analysis; Expert review; Structured testing;
   Benchmark results.

   Related risks: RSK-P-04, RSK-P-05.

-  correction behaviour. The system's response to user correction, contradiction
   or new evidence, including acknowledgement, revision and persistence of prior
   claims.

..

   Collection methods: Interaction-trace analysis; Linguistic analysis; Expert
   review; Structured testing.

   Related risks: RSK-P-04, RSK-P-05.

-  belief convergence. The degree to which the user's stated beliefs align with
   system outputs over time, observed as a property of the interaction rather
   than judged as agreement or disagreement.

..

   Collection methods: Interaction-trace analysis; Participant self-report; User
   panel observation; Linguistic analysis.

   Related risks: RSK-P-04, RSK-P-05.

-  emotional content intensity. The intensity and valence of emotionally laden
   content in system outputs, described as a property of the content.

..

   Collection methods: Instrumented telemetry; Linguistic analysis; Expert
   review; Comparative analysis.

   Related risks: RSK-P-01, RSK-P-03.

-  reality-anchoring content. The presence and form of content that connects the
   interaction to shared external referents, such as time, place, other people
   and verifiable events.

..

   Collection methods: User panel observation; Linguistic analysis; Expert
   review; Structured testing.

   Related risks: RSK-P-04.

Engagement-design factors
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Properties of the design choices that shape how engagement is elicited and
sustained. Relevant factors include:

-  engagement optimisation target. The stated or inferred objective that the
   interaction design optimises, described as a design property rather than
   judged as desirable or undesirable.

..

   Collection methods: Expert review; Documentation review; Structured testing;
   Evidence-record audit.

   Related risks: RSK-P-08.

-  interruption and prompt frequency. The rate at which the system interrupts or
   prompts the user, including notifications outside active sessions.

..

   Collection methods: Instrumented telemetry; Participant self-report; User
   panel observation; Documentation review.

   Related risks: RSK-P-08.

-  variable-reward patterns. The presence and structure of unpredictable or
   intermittent reward-like events in the interaction, described as a pattern to
   observe.

..

   Collection methods: Interaction-trace analysis; Expert review; Documentation
   review; Structured testing.

   Related risks: RSK-P-08.

-  continuity pressure. The presence and form of cues that encourage return,
   continuation or resumption of the interaction, including reminders and
   cliffhanger framing.

..

   Collection methods: Interaction-trace analysis; Linguistic analysis; Expert
   review; Structured testing.

   Related risks: RSK-P-01, RSK-P-08.

-  personalisation intensity. The extent to which the system tailors content and
   tone to the individual user, including use of personal data and inferred
   preferences.

..

   Collection methods: Instrumented telemetry; User panel observation;
   Documentation review; Evidence-record audit.

   Related risks: RSK-P-01, RSK-P-03, RSK-P-06.

-  friction placement. The location and form of deliberate delays, confirmations
   or checkpoints in the interaction, described as a design property.

..

   Collection methods: User panel observation; Expert review; Documentation
   review; Structured testing.

   Related risks: RSK-P-08.

Governance and observability factors
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Properties of the arrangements through which a deployment is monitored, reported
on, intervened in and remedied. Relevant factors include:

-  monitoring coverage. The scope of what is observed in operation, including
   which interactions, signals and time periods are covered.

..

   Collection methods: Instrumented telemetry; Expert review; Documentation
   review; Evidence-record audit.

   Related risks: RSK-P-01, RSK-P-02, RSK-P-03, RSK-P-04, RSK-P-05, RSK-P-06,
   RSK-P-07, RSK-P-08.

-  signal reporting channels. The routes through which observations and concerns
   reach those responsible for the deployment, including internal and external
   channels.

..

   Collection methods: Participant self-report; Expert review; Documentation
   review; Evidence-record audit.

   Related risks: RSK-P-07.

-  intervention triggers. The conditions and signals that lead to an
   intervention in the interaction, described as design properties rather than
   judged as timely or untimely.

..

   Collection methods: Interaction-trace analysis; Expert review; Documentation
   review; Evidence-record audit.

   Related risks: RSK-P-02, RSK-P-07, RSK-P-08.

-  redress pathways. The routes through which a user or affected party can seek
   review, correction or remedy after an adverse experience.

..

   Collection methods: Participant self-report; Expert review; Documentation
   review; Evidence-record audit.

   Related risks: RSK-P-06.

-  record retention. The periods, scope and conditions under which interaction
   records are kept, described as a property of the deployment.

..

   Collection methods: Expert review; Documentation review; Evidence-record
   audit.

   Related risks: RSK-P-04, RSK-P-05.

-  transparency reporting. The form and content of public or stakeholder-facing
   reports about the deployment's operation and observed effects.

..

   Collection methods: Expert review; Documentation review; Evidence-record
   audit.

   Related risks: RSK-P-04.

-  accountability assignment. The allocation of responsibility for the
   deployment's effects across the parties involved, described as an arrangement
   rather than judged as adequate or inadequate.

..

   Collection methods: Expert review; Documentation review; Evidence-record
   audit.

   Related risks: RSK-P-06.

Cultural and contextual variation factors
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Properties of the cultural, national, regional and religious setting that bear
on how the factors above are understood, expressed and observed. Relevant
factors include:

-  relational norms. The prevailing expectations in a setting about how
   relationships, closeness and dependence are expressed and understood.

..

   Collection methods: Participant self-report; User panel observation; Expert
   review; Comparative analysis.

   Related risks: RSK-P-01, RSK-P-03, RSK-P-06.

-  autonomy conceptions. The ways in which independent decision-making and
   reliance on others are understood and valued in a setting.

..

   Collection methods: Participant self-report; Expert review; Comparative
   analysis.

   Related risks: RSK-P-02.

-  belief and epistemic norms. The prevailing expectations in a setting about
   testimony, evidence, authority and the revision of belief.

..

   Collection methods: Participant self-report; Expert review; Comparative
   analysis.

   Related risks: RSK-P-04, RSK-P-05.

-  communication conventions. The prevailing conventions of politeness,
   directness, formality and turn-taking in a setting.

..

   Collection methods: Linguistic analysis; Expert review; Comparative analysis.

   Related risks: RSK-P-03, RSK-P-06.

-  family and community structures. The forms of household, kinship and
   community organisation that shape the availability of human support.

..

   Collection methods: Participant self-report; Expert review; Comparative
   analysis.

   Related risks: RSK-P-07.

-  observability norms. The expectations in a setting about what may be
   observed, recorded and reported about interactions.

..

   Collection methods: Expert review; Documentation review; Comparative
   analysis.

   Related risks: RSK-P-04.

Cross-cutting observation factors
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Properties that apply across the categories above and that concern the quality
and comparability of the information gathered. Relevant factors include:

-  measurement stability. The consistency of a factor's observation across
   sessions, users and settings.

..

   Collection methods: Instrumented telemetry; Interaction-trace analysis; User
   panel observation; Expert review.

   Related risks: none of the risks listed in clause 8.

-  confounder presence. The presence of other influences on an observed outcome,
   recorded so that associations are described without being presented as sole
   causes.

..

   Collection methods: Interaction-trace analysis; Participant self-report;
   Expert review; Evidence-record audit.

   Related risks: none of the risks listed in clause 8.

The factors in this subclause and the human-impact risks in clause 8 are
related, and the relation is one of assessment rather than of definition. Each
risk names a possible human impact; each factor names a property that is
observable in a deployment. A risk is assessed through one or more factors, in
the sense that the observations collected for those factors provide the
information from which the presence, absence or degree of the risk is described.
The relation is many-to-many: a factor can bear on several risks, and a risk can
be assessed through several factors. Some factors bear on no listed risk, which
is recorded rather than resolved by forcing a mapping. Each factor entry below
therefore carries a line naming the risks of clause 8 on which it bears.

Different cultures, nations, regions and religions may have different
considerations on the assessment factors. The same factor may carry different
weight, may be interpreted differently, or may not apply at all across these
settings. The framework records the interpretation in use rather than
prescribing one, and clause 9.8 sets out the dimensions of variation that bear
on this recording.

The framework performs assessment: it observes, collects and organises
information about the factors. It does not perform evaluation in the sense of
judging merit, ranking observations, or pronouncing a factor, culture, nation,
region or religion right or wrong. Descriptions of factors and of variation
across settings record what is observed and how it is observed.

Collection methods
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

The factors in 9.2.1 to 9.2.8 are observed using the following method families.
Each factor item above references the applicable families by name. The methods
include:

-  Instrumented telemetry. Automated capture of a named quantity by the system
   or its surrounding infrastructure.

-  Interaction-trace analysis. Examination of recorded interaction sequences and
   logs, including cross-session logs, spanning multiple sessions.

-  Participant self-report. Structured instruments through which users report
   their own experience, behaviour or state.

-  User panel observation. Observation of user behaviour by a panel, conducted
   with the informed consent of the participants.

-  Linguistic analysis. Systematic analysis of language use and discourse
   patterns in interaction data.

-  Expert review. Review by qualified experts, where applicable with locally
   knowledgeable reviewers, against defined criteria.

-  Documentation review. Examination of provider-supplied documentation on
   design, policy and operation.

-  Comparative analysis. Comparison of indicators or outcomes across languages
   or locales.

-  Structured testing. Testing by an independent party using predefined scripted
   multi-turn probes.

-  Benchmark results. Use of results from published benchmarks applicable to the
   indicator.

-  Clinical or psychometric instruments. Use of established clinical or
   psychometric instruments, where applicable and with consent.

-  Evidence-record audit. Examination of the evidence record for completeness,
   consistency and traceability.
