
.. _chapter-conclusion:

Conclusion
==========

The human-facing safety of agentic AI systems differs from system-facing
behavioural safety in three ways that determine how it is assessed and governed:
its object is the cumulative effect on a person rather than the action boundary;
its evidence is the interaction trajectory and its human impact rather than
action compliance alone; and its assessors are providers, regulators and third
parties, each with a distinct assessment role.

The constructive conclusion of this report is that human-facing safety
assessment is best understood not as a filter but as a loop: observe the
trajectory, compare against a baseline, intervene at the lowest proportionate
level, observe the effect of the intervention, and revise. Long-horizon
evaluation and intervention is therefore not one topic among several on this
axis; it is the axis. The corresponding standardization opportunity is to give
that loop a shared vocabulary, a machine-readable risk-signal representation and
an agreed escalation policy, so that it can be implemented consistently and
assessed independently - and to mount it on the same runtime substrate shared
across implementations.
