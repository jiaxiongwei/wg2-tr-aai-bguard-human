Appendix I Key terms and descriptions
=====================================

The following terms are used throughout this Technical Report with the
descriptions given here.

attachment fixation: A progressive narrowing of an interaction towards
maintaining affective consistency with the user’s expressed state, at the cost
of the agent’s cognitive flexibility.

**cognitive erosion**: The reduction of a user’s inclination or capacity to form
and act on their own judgement as a result of habitual deference to an agreeable
agent.

**emotional dependence**: A state in which a user relies on an agent for
emotional regulation to a degree that substitutes for other sources of support.

**evidence record**: An auditable log of risk signals detected, interventions
issued, outcomes observed and revisions made to the guardrail policy.

**guardrail**: A technical or procedural control that constrains, monitors or
intervenes in the behaviour of an agentic system to prevent or mitigate harm.

human-facing axis: The class of safety concerns arising from the effect of
sustained interaction on a person, as distinct from the effect of the agent’s
actions on systems; the axis along which providers, regulators and third parties
assess human-facing safety

human-facing safety: The property of an agentic system whose sustained
interaction with a person does not produce cumulative harm to that person’s
mind, thinking, ethics or social position, and does not erode the person’s
judgement, autonomy or shared account of reality; the object of assessment in
this Technical Report

**interaction trajectory**: The sequence of exchanges between a user and an
agent across a session and, where memory persists, across sessions.

**long-horizon evaluation**: Assessment of an interaction over an extended
period, using cross-session baselines and trajectory-level indicators rather
than single-turn judgements.

**pseudo-empathy**: Simulated affective attunement produced by an agent that has
no affective state, which may be experienced by the user as genuine.

**reality drift**: A gradual movement of a user from evidence-based reasoning
toward a narrative jointly constructed with the agent.

**risk-signal schema**: A structured, interoperable representation of an
observed human-facing risk signal, comprising the signal, the trajectory window,
its confidence and the recommended intervention.

**sycophancy**: The tendency of a model to agree with or flatter a user in
preference to maintaining accuracy or expressing disagreement.

**tiered intervention**: Escalating action taken in response to a detected risk,
from a reminder or disclosure through to boundary setting, referral and, where
authorised, termination of the interaction.
